Wristful

Privacy Policy

This policy covers the app "Wristful" for iPhone and Apple Watch, and its pages on this website (treeyears.app/wristful). It explains what happens to personal data under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Last updated: 11 November 2026

In short

  • Your meditations, history, reminders and settings stay on your iPhone and Apple Watch, and in Apple Health if you allow it. I don't receive, see or store them.
  • There's no account, no tracking, no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party code.
  • Wristful doesn't go online. Your iPhone and your watch talk only to each other.
  • It asks for two permissions: Apple Health, to save your meditations and read your heart rate and Mindful Minutes, and notifications, only if you set a reminder. It asks for no other: no location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • Apple runs the App Store, TestFlight and Siri. Apple passes me crash reports only if you've agreed to share them with app developers.

2. What Wristful keeps on your devices

  • Your meditations: the ones in your library, with their names, lengths, interval cues and rounds, and which one is your usual meditation.
  • Your history: for each finished meditation, its name, when it started and ended, how long it was planned and how long you sat, its rounds, whether it ended early, and your heart rate during it as average, lowest and highest.
  • Your reminders: their times, their text, and how they skip a day you've already meditated.
  • Your settings: the strength of the cues, the get-ready time, whether meditations are recorded as workouts, and whether you've finished the first-run screens.
  • A meditation in progress: while one runs, the watch keeps its state, so it can carry on if the app is closed.

These are stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone and your watch, which other apps can't read. The watch's complication, control and Smart Stack card read from the watch's storage; the Live Activity gets what it shows from the iPhone app. Your iPhone and your watch exchange your library, settings, reminder times, finished meditations and the state of a running meditation directly with each other, through Apple's connection between paired devices (WatchConnectivity).

Wristful doesn't send any of this to me or to anyone else, and I have no access to it. Storing it on your devices is strictly necessary to provide the timer you asked for, so the app doesn't ask for consent (§ 25(2) no. 2 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act, TDDDG).

How long, and how to delete it: a meditation stays in your history until you delete it in History, or until you delete the app. Deleting the app removes its data from that device; delete it on both iPhone and watch to remove everything. Copies in backups remain until those backups are replaced or deleted (see section 6).

3. Apple Health

Wristful works with Apple Health only if you allow it, in the permission sheet that iOS and watchOS show. You can choose each kind of data separately.

  • What it saves: each meditation of a minute or longer, as Mindful Minutes, with the meditation's name and its planned and actual minutes. If you turn on "Record as workout", it also saves a Mind & Body workout, with the heart rate and energy the watch measured during it; Apple Fitness shows it with your workouts.
  • What it reads: your heart rate during your meditations, to show it in History; your Mindful Minutes, so a reminder can skip a day you've already meditated, and to rebuild your history on a new iPhone; and the entries it saved itself, to delete them when you delete a meditation in History.

Health data stays in Apple Health. Wristful reads it only on your devices and never sends it anywhere. Apple keeps Health data encrypted on your devices, and syncs it through iCloud only if you've turned that on, end-to-end encrypted (Health & Privacy).

Heart rate and Mindful Minutes are data concerning health (Art. 9 GDPR). Wristful processes them only with your explicit consent, which you give in the permission sheet (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time, for each kind of data, in the iPhone's Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Wristful, or in the Health app. Withdrawing doesn't affect what happened before. The entries Wristful saved stay in Health until you delete them there, or in Wristful's History.

4. Reminders

If you set a reminder, the app asks iOS for permission to send you notifications, and schedules them for the times you chose, with the text you gave them. The notifications are made and kept on your iPhone by iOS; nothing is sent to me or to a server. Meditate now in a reminder starts your usual meditation on your watch. Your watch keeps the reminder times, without their text, to offer your usual meditation in the Smart Stack around them.

You can change or remove reminders in the app's Settings, and turn off notifications in the iPhone's Settings.

5. Siri, Shortcuts, widgets and the Live Activity

  • Siri and Shortcuts. If you start a meditation by asking Siri, Apple processes your request under its own privacy policy (Siri & Privacy). Wristful only learns which meditation to start. The names of your meditations are shared with Siri on your device, so it can understand them.
  • The complication, the Smart Stack card, the Control Center control and the Live Activity show a meditation's name and times on your watch face and your iPhone's Lock Screen. They're made on your devices. Anyone who looks at your watch or Lock Screen can see them; you can remove them, or turn off Live Activities for Wristful in the iPhone's Settings.

6. Backups and iCloud

Like other apps' data, Wristful's data on your iPhone is part of the iPhone's backups: in iCloud Backup if you've turned it on, or in a backup on your computer. That includes your history, library, reminders and settings; what the watch keeps comes back from the iPhone when they connect. Apple keeps iCloud Backups under its own privacy policy; with Advanced Data Protection turned on, they're end-to-end encrypted (iCloud data security overview). I have no access to them. In your iCloud settings you can choose whether this app is included in iCloud Backup.

Wristful doesn't sync through iCloud itself. Apple Health syncs your Health data, including what Wristful saved, if you've turned on Health in iCloud.

7. Apple: App Store, crash reports and TestFlight

Apple is a separate controller for its own services and processes data under its own privacy policy. In the European Economic Area, the controller is Apple Distribution International Ltd. in Ireland. See the Apple Privacy Policy and App Store & Privacy.

  • Downloads and updates. Apple processes your Apple Account and download details. I don't receive them. Apple gives me statistics that don't identify you, such as the number of downloads per country. Reviews you write on the App Store are public, and I can reply to them there.
  • Crash reports and usage statistics. Only if you've turned on "Share With App Developers" (Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple sends me crash reports and usage statistics for the app. A crash report contains technical details such as the iPhone or watch model, the system and app versions, and what the app was doing when it crashed. Apple provides them in a form that doesn't identify you (App Analytics & Privacy). I use them only to find and fix bugs, based on my legitimate interest in an app that works (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). I don't keep copies longer than it takes to fix the bug. You can turn the sharing off at any time in the same setting.
  • TestFlight (beta testers only). If you test a beta version through TestFlight, Apple shares with me: the name and email address I invited you with (not if you joined through a public link), whether you accepted the invitation and installed the app, your device model, system version and language, the number of sessions and crashes, crash logs, and any feedback you send (your comment, screenshots, and technical details such as battery level, free storage, time zone and connection type). I use this only to run the test and improve the app, and I don't pass it on. This is based on your taking part in the test (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and on my legitimate interest in fixing bugs (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). I remove testers and their details when the test ends or when they ask; Apple keeps feedback for one year (TestFlight & Privacy).

10. No tracking, no advertising, no third-party code

The app contains no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting code from other companies, and no other third-party code. It has no account and no social media buttons. It doesn't track you across apps or websites, doesn't use your advertising identifier, and doesn't sell or share your data with anyone. It uses only Apple's frameworks that are part of iOS and watchOS.

11. Children

Wristful is meant for everyone. Since it collects no personal data from anyone, it collects none from children either.
